Structure 09 · Brazil

Hercílio Luz Bridge

Strait between Santa Catarina Island and the mainland, Florianópolis, Santa Catarina, Brazil · 1922–1926

Hanging over the sea, with steel shipped from the United States and money the state did not have, a bridge with a 339.5-meter span held up by chains of steel bars that, if one of them broke, might give no warning.

Until 1926, anyone who wanted to leave Santa Catarina Island, where downtown Florianópolis sits, crossed by boat. When the strong south wind blew, they did not cross. Governor Hercílio Luz wanted a bridge to end the capital's isolation, at a time when some were arguing for moving the seat of government to Lages, up on the plateau.

The solution, designed in New York by the firm Robinson & Steinman, was a suspension bridge in which the cable is not a cable: it is a chain of steel bars with an eye at each end, joined by pins, which in the middle of the span becomes part of the truss itself. Of the three solutions compared, it was the cheapest way to cross 339.5 meters over the sea. It was also the kind of bridge that, in 1967, in the United States, collapsed entirely when a single bar broke.

01An island waiting on the wind

Florianópolis was born on Santa Catarina Island, separated from the mainland by a strait a few hundred meters wide. At the start of the twentieth century, everything the island did not produce arrived by boat: cattle, food, people. When the south wind blew, the crossing stopped. According to journalist Maurício Oliveira, the folklorist Franklin Cascaes recalled that this meant at least three days without boats.

The isolation also had a political price. Some argued for moving the capital to Lages, up on the plateau. Hercílio Luz, born in Desterro in 1860 and back in the state governorship, made the bridge his priority. He wanted to call it the Independence Bridge and, according to Maurício Oliveira, to open it on the centennial of September 7, 1922; the loans were delayed, and work only began in November of that year.

02The chain that becomes a truss

The first design, by the São Paulo builder Byington & Sundstrom, was a truss bridge, too expensive for the state. In 1920, according to the history written by Professor Narbal Marcellino, of UFSC, the builder consulted the American engineer Holton D. Robinson, who brought in David B. Steinman. The two compared three solutions and settled on the most economical: a suspension bridge in which the cable is a chain of eyebars, flat steel bars with a hole at each end, linked to one another by pins.

Chains like that were nineteenth-century technology, already replaced almost everywhere by steel wire cables. What Steinman did was put them to work twice. Between one quarter and three quarters of the span, the chain comes down to meet the truss that stiffens the deck and becomes its top chord; toward the ends, the truss hangs from the chain on vertical hangers. Less steel in the girder, fewer hangers, less load. The American Bridge Company supplied bars of heat-treated steel, and the towers were designed to rock on pinned bearings at the base. The bridge was designed to carry a road, an electric railway line and the city's water main.

03Everything by ship

All the material came from the United States, and, according to architect Joel Pacheco, the ships were too large to dock near the island: the cargo was transferred to smaller boats. The construction yard was set up on the mainland, because at the island end there was a cemetery, later moved to Itacorubi.

The foundations, with steel-cased piles driven down to the rock, were finished in 1924. In July of that year the American erection crew arrived. The mainland tower went up in six weeks, by December 1924; the island tower, in five, by February 1925. In April 1925 the chains were closed, and in August the main structure was complete. At the peak, according to engineer Wenceslau Diotallevy, between 381 and 400 people were working there. How many passed through the job in all, and whether anyone died on it, the sources consulted do not record.

Hercílio Luz did not see any of it finished. Ill with cancer, he inaugurated a wooden replica of the bridge in the city center on October 8, 1924. He died twelve days later. The real bridge was opened on May 13, 1926, in the rain, by acting governor Antônio Vicente Bulcão Viana, still without the wooden walkway on the main span. It bore the name of Hercílio Luz.

04A fifty-year bill

The money came from two loans of $5 million each, taken out in 1919 and 1922 with New York bankers. According to the reconstruction published in the state's Official Gazette in 1935, they yielded 20,263 contos de réis, but 12,032 contos were eaten up by fees, commissions and other expenses. That left 8,231. The first bank went bankrupt, and the state ended up liable for debts of the failed institution. The final cost, which circulates in different and poorly written versions, is described as practically double Santa Catarina's annual budget.

To pay for it, a toll was charged: 100 réis per person, with surcharges for vehicles and baggage, from 1926 to 1935. According to Maurício Oliveira, in the second half of 1929 the bridge brought in 97 contos, about 1% of the state's revenue. The toll fell under popular pressure. The loans were only paid off in 1978.

05The sister bridge that fell

The Hercílio Luz had two close relatives in the United States, both over the Ohio River: the Silver Bridge and the St. Marys bridge, of the same eyebar-chain type. On December 15, 1967, at rush hour, the Silver Bridge collapsed all at once and killed 46 people. The cause was a fracture in the eye of a single bar, corroded from the inside over four decades. The St. Marys bridge was dismantled. The one in Florianópolis, the oldest of the three, became the only one left.

In Florianópolis, the news hastened a new bridge, the Colombo Salles, opened in 1975. But in 1969 the Hercílio Luz's wooden deck had already been replaced with heavier asphalt, and it was still carrying almost half the traffic. A report by IPT in São Paulo pointed to deteriorated bars, and a crack appeared in the eye of a bar near the top of a tower: a crack five centimeters long, according to the UFSC history; a UFMG technical study speaks of the bar fracturing. The bridge was closed on January 22, 1982. It reopened to pedestrians and bicycles in 1988 and was closed for good in 1991. In 1995, a report by the state highway department concluded that the bars could cause the bridge to collapse abruptly and could not be reused.

06Replacing the chains of a suspension bridge

To replace the chain of a bridge that hangs from it, the weight first has to be taken off the chain. The solution was to build under the main span a temporary structure with its own foundation, and to jack the deck up with hydraulic jacks until the bars carried no load. The first transfer of weight was made in the early hours of February 12, 2017, with traffic closed and nearby residents put up in hotels. In November of that year, the chains between the towers were removed.

The road to that point had taken more than a decade: the first stage, on the approach viaducts, began in 2006; the second was contracted in 2008 and had its contract terminated in 2014, followed by court battles. In 2011, the diver Sandro José Erben Saes drowned while inspecting a pile of the temporary foundation. The American Bridge Company, invited in 2015, turned the job down. The Portuguese group Teixeira Duarte took over in 2016.

According to NSC's series on the centennial, the new bars, 360 of them, about 800 tons, were made by Usiminas, in Minas Gerais; the saddles at the tops of the towers came from Spain, and the hangers from Italy. The first new bar was installed in March 2018. In August 2019, the bridge was once again held up by the chains alone. The cost of it all, from 1981 to the lighting, exceeds half a billion reais by NSC's count; Diário Catarinense arrived at no less than R$618 million in inflation-adjusted terms through 2018.

07A hundred years and two nights a week

The Hercílio Luz reopened on December 30, 2019, before more than 200,000 people, with a steel grating deck in place of the asphalt. It got decorative lighting in December 2024 and turned 100 on May 13, 2026, with three weeks of celebrations organized by the state government and the city.

Two months later, on July 28, 2026, the government announced that the bridge would close to cars every Tuesday and Thursday, from 20:00 to midnight, indefinitely, for welding, tightening and adjustment work on the deck and the grating. Pedestrians and cyclists could still cross. In October 2026, the bridge that had been closed for 28 years so it would not fall was closing two nights a week so it could stay open.

MYTHSWhat people say and what the record shows

  • What people say

    It was the largest suspension bridge in the world when it was finished.

    What the record shows

    No. In 1926 there were cable suspension bridges with longer spans. What it had was the longest eyebar-chain suspension span in the world at the time. Today it is the largest suspension bridge in Brazil and the largest eyebar suspension bridge still standing.

  • What people say

    It is a bridge unique in the world.

    What the record shows

    It was one of three bridges of nearly the same design: the other two, the Silver Bridge and the St. Marys bridge, stood over the Ohio River, in the United States. The Silver Bridge fell in 1967 and the St. Marys bridge was dismantled. The one in Florianópolis is the oldest of the three and the only one left.

  • What people say

    Hercílio Luz opened the bridge that bears his name.

    What the record shows

    He died in October 1924, twelve days after symbolically inaugurating a wooden replica. The bridge was opened on May 13, 1926, by the acting governor, Antônio Vicente Bulcão Viana.

  • What people say

    The bridge was built to carry trains.

    What the record shows

    It was designed to carry an electric railway line and the city's water main as well. No railway line was ever installed on it.

QUESTIONSFrequently asked questions

When was the Hercílio Luz Bridge built?

Work began in November 1922 and the bridge was opened on May 13, 1926, in the rain. The foundations were finished in 1924, and the towers went up between December 1924 and February 1925.

How long is the Hercílio Luz Bridge?

The main suspension span is 339.5 m over the sea. The total length is 821 m according to the state government or 819.5 m according to the UFMG study, and the towers stand 74.21 or 75 m above the sea, depending on the source.

Who built the Hercílio Luz Bridge?

The design is by the firm Robinson & Steinman, of New York. Byington & Sundstrom, of São Paulo, built the foundations, and the steel, all of it from the United States, was fabricated and erected by the American Bridge Company.

Why was the Hercílio Luz Bridge closed?

It was closed on January 22, 1982, after a report by IPT and the discovery of a crack in the eye of a bar, the same kind of failure that brought down the Silver Bridge in 1967. Closed for good in 1991, it only reopened on December 30, 2019, after receiving 360 new bars.

Was the Hercílio Luz Bridge the largest suspension bridge in the world?

No. In 1926 there were cable suspension bridges with longer spans; it had the longest eyebar-chain suspension span in the world at the time. Today it is the largest suspension bridge in Brazil.
